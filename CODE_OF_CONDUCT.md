# codemcp Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We want codemcp to be a welcoming, constructive, and harassment-free project for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

This Code of Conduct sets out what we expect from participants, where and to whom it applies, how to report problems, and what happens when the rules are broken. It exists to protect contributors and users, and also the maintainers who give their time to the project.

## Our Standards

Behavior that contributes to a healthy community:

* Being kind, patient, and empathetic toward others
* Respecting differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Taking responsibility for our mistakes, apologizing to those affected, and learning from them
* Helping newcomers get started with the project and its tooling
* Focusing on what is best for the community as a whole

Behavior that is not acceptable:

* Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances
* Trolling, insults, derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information (such as a physical or email address) without explicit permission
* Sustained disruption of discussions, issues, or pull requests
* Spam, or submitting malicious code or deliberately harmful contributions
* Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all spaces managed by the project, including:

* The GitHub repository: issues, pull requests, code reviews, discussions, and comments
* Any other official project channels (chat, mailing lists, social media accounts)
* Public spaces where someone is officially representing the project, such as using a project email address or speaking at an event on its behalf

It also applies to conduct outside these spaces when it targets a project participant or otherwise harms the community.

## Who This Applies To

Everyone who participates in the project: maintainers, contributors, reviewers, users reporting issues, sponsors, and anyone else interacting with the project or its community. Maintainers are held to the same standards as everyone else.

## Reporting a Violation

If you experience or witness unacceptable behavior, please report it privately by emailing **mahesh.chopade.tech@gmail.com**. Reports go to Mahesh Chopade, the project maintainer, who is responsible for enforcement.

Please include, where you can:

* What happened, and when
* Where it happened (links to issues, comments, or pull requests are helpful)
* Who was involved, and any witnesses
* Anything else that would help us understand the situation

You can report something even if you are unsure whether it technically breaks a rule. We would rather hear about a problem early.

## How We Handle Reports

* **Every report is taken seriously.** We treat each community member's account as seriously as our own and will investigate even if it differs from our own experience with the person involved.
* **We gather information first.** Maintainers will review the relevant conversations and history, and seek other perspectives where appropriate, before deciding anything.
* **Your privacy is protected.** Reports are kept confidential and shared only with those who need to know to act on them. Maintainers handle direct communication with the person in question, and will not ask you to confront them unless you request it.
* **We will follow up.** You will be told the outcome of your report. If we decide the behavior was not a violation, we will explain why. Behavior that does not strictly break a rule may still be a problem, and we may talk to the person involved or revise this Code of Conduct to make expectations clearer.
* **The process is fair.** The person whose behavior is reported will be given the chance to understand the concern and respond, and decisions will be applied consistently.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will respond with the action they judge fair and proportionate to the situation. Possible responses, roughly in order of severity:

1. **Private conversation.** A private message explaining how the behavior affected others and what is expected going forward. The reporter may be copied, with their consent, so they know action was taken.
2. **Public warning.** A clear but kind public note, ideally in the place where it happened, explaining the impact of the behavior. This shows the community that the Code of Conduct is taken seriously.
3. **Temporary suspension.** A temporary ban from participating in any part of the project, for a stated period.
4. **Permanent ban.** Permanent removal from the project community. This is reserved for cases where a resolution is not possible, such as repeated or serious violations, harassment, or hostility when the issue is raised.

Maintainers may also remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, pull requests, and other contributions that violate this Code of Conduct.

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for upholding these standards fairly and evenly, for following the process described here, and for acting on reports promptly. Maintainers who violate this Code of Conduct are subject to the same enforcement.
